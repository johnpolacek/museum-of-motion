# Finding the vibe

Read this when the feel of the motion isn't settled yet: a full treatment, a new project, or a user who describes a mood ("make it feel calm", "something premium", "more fun"). Skip it for a named effect: "add a magnetic button" goes straight to its recipe. When the user asks for the `style-animaxxing` look, that skill's direction already sets the feel; skip this too.

Before choosing effects, work out two things.

**What the project feels like.** Read it from what is already there: the brand, the audience, the content, the tone of the copy, the existing design. A children's reading app and a law firm can share a layout and still want opposite motion.

**What the user wants from animation.** Motion can guide attention, give feedback, set a mood, tell a story, or simply delight. It can be a quiet layer under the content or the main event. "Add some animation" can mean either, so find out which, and how much.

Then let the feel shape the choices: how long things take, how they settle, how far they travel, whether they arrive together or in turn, how presses and hovers answer, and whether anything moves on its own. Pick a moment or two to carry the feeling and keep the rest quiet. These are questions to weigh, not settings to apply: two calm sites can still move differently.

Ask at most one question, and only when the cues are thin or disagree. If the scope is also unclear, fold both into the one question the skill already asks. Then say what you found and what you plan in a line or two, so the user can redirect before you build.
